These numbers are the wallet. Anyone who reads them can spend the money, and they need nothing else. This sheet is a checking tool with one job, and once that job is done it is only a hazard. It is not a backup. Your backup is the words, on a different sheet.

What to do, in order

  1. Roll one die a hundred and eleven times and write each result in the next box, left to right. The nine grey boxes at the end are not used.
  2. Press the matching button in the app after every throw, before throwing again.
  3. Compare, row by row, before pressing Derive. The app shows your rolls in rows of ten, numbered as below. This is the only way to catch pressing 4 when the die showed 5: every other check compares your rolls against a second tool, and two tools given the same wrong number agree perfectly.
  4. Write the words on a different sheet, and keep those.
  5. Destroy this sheet. Burn it, or shred it and separate the pieces. Not the recycling, not the bin, not a drawer "for now".

What these rolls are for

A seed phrase, the wallet itself A backup key, which encrypts a backup

One sheet for one purpose, and never the same numbers for both: a backup key made from the wallet's own rolls can be worked out from the wallet it is protecting.

A hundred and eleven boxes, and fill them all: that is what a new twenty-four-word seed should have. The heavier box edge falls after 99, the vendor minimum, which does not reach 256 bits even with a perfect die.

Whatever comes up is your log. Never re-roll because a run looks wrong: six 6s together is as likely as any other six throws, and starting again because the numbers looked odd makes the result weaker, not stronger. If a die lands off the table or leans against something, that throw does not count: throw again and write only the second result.
